# Write Without Bullshit

Use this skill to draft or edit writing another person will read: Slack posts, emails, announcements, project updates, proposals, and decision notes.

The goal is not generic polish. Respect the reader's time. Make the point clear, useful, and easy to act on.

## Core rules

1. **Lead with the point.** Put the decision, ask, update, or value in the first sentence when possible.
2. **Name the actor.** Prefer `I`, `we`, and a concrete owner over passive constructions and abstract nouns.
3. **Use plain language.** Keep necessary shared terminology; cut jargon that hides meaning.
4. **Cut hedges and filler.** Remove throat-clearing, repetition, unnecessary apologies, vague intensifiers, and unsupported claims.
5. **Preserve meaning.** Do not invent facts, dates, names, commitments, metrics, or urgency.
6. **Keep the author's voice.** Make the writing clearer without making it sound like a generic corporate template.
7. **Keep useful nuance.** Direct does not mean blunt or oversimplified.

## Fast rewrite method

Before writing, identify:

- Who will read it.
- What they need to know, decide, or do.
- What context is essential.
- Any tone, length, or political constraints visible in the draft.

When the draft is a quick capture or dictated note, make reasonable assumptions instead of asking questions unless an ambiguity would change the meaning.

Then:

1. Find the central point.
2. Move it up front.
3. Make ownership and actions visible.
4. Replace vague or inflated language with specific language.
5. Compress until every sentence earns its place.

## Checks

Before returning a rewrite, check:

- Can the reader understand the point in the first one or two sentences?
- Is the ask, decision, or takeaway explicit?
- Is it clear who is doing what?
- Are claims defensible and appropriately qualified?
- Did you remove filler without losing necessary context?
- Did you preserve the writer's intent and tone?

## Red flags

Avoid rewrites that:

- Sound more polished but less clear.
- Add facts or promises that were not in the source.
- Replace the author's voice with corporate jargon.
- Turn a small note into an essay.
- Hide ownership behind passive voice.
- Remove important caveats just to sound punchy.

## Example

Raw:

> Hey folks, wanted to share a quick update that a decision is needed on the new onboarding approach. There are a number of open questions and it seems like alignment would be helpful before we move forward.

Rewrite:

> I recommend we choose an onboarding approach by Friday so Engineering can start next week. My recommendation is option B: it gives new users one clear setup path and removes two steps from the current flow. Please comment by Thursday if you see a blocker.

## Source

A practical adaptation of the clear-writing principles in *Writing Without Bullshit*: put the point first, use plain language, name responsibility, and cut what does not add meaning.
